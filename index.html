01 — UNDERSTANDING

Clear information builds confidence

Cancer is a group of diseases involving abnormal cell growth. Different cancers have different causes, risk factors, symptoms, and screening approaches.

🔬

What is cancer?

It occurs when abnormal cells grow uncontrollably and may invade nearby tissues or spread to other parts of the body.

🫁

Many different types

Breast, lung, cervical, colorectal, prostate, oral, liver, and skin cancers are among the many distinct types.

🌍

Community awareness

Reliable information can help people understand preventable risks and make informed decisions about their health.

02 — RISK FACTORS

Know what can influence risk

Risk factors can be lifestyle-related, environmental, infection-related, or inherited. Their effects vary from person to person.

🚭

Tobacco

Tobacco use and exposure to tobacco smoke are important preventable risks.

🍷

Alcohol

Alcohol consumption is associated with increased risk of several cancers.

🥗

Diet and body weight

Balanced eating habits and a healthy body weight support overall health.

🏃

Physical inactivity

Regular activity appropriate to a person's circumstances supports healthy living.

☀️

UV and environment

Excessive ultraviolet exposure and some environmental or occupational exposures matter.

🦠

Certain infections

HPV, hepatitis B and C, and some other infections are linked to certain cancers.

Remember: Having a risk factor does not mean that a person has cancer. Some factors, such as age, family history, and genetics, cannot be changed.
03 — PREVENTION

Everyday choices that support health

Prevention is about practical, evidence-informed habits and reducing avoidable exposures where possible.

01

Avoid tobacco

Avoid tobacco products and second-hand smoke; seek qualified support if you want help quitting.

02

Choose balanced meals

Include vegetables, fruit, whole grains, and legumes as part of a varied diet.

03

Stay active

Build regular physical activity into your routine in ways that fit your age and circumstances.

04

Protect against UV

Use suitable sun protection and avoid excessive ultraviolet exposure.

05

Limit alcohol

Reducing alcohol consumption can lower exposure to an established cancer risk factor.

06

Ask about vaccination

Discuss HPV and hepatitis B vaccination with a qualified healthcare professional.

07

Reduce harmful exposures

Follow relevant safety guidance for environmental and occupational exposures.

08

Use recommended screening

Ask a healthcare professional which screening options are appropriate for you.

09

Share trusted information

Help friends and family find reliable health guidance and professional care.

04 — EARLY ACTION

Notice changes. Seek trusted advice.

Persistent or unusual changes are a reason to speak with a qualified healthcare professional. Screening guidance depends on the person and the type of cancer.

🔴

A new or changing lump

🩸

Unusual bleeding

🫁

A persistent cough or hoarseness

⚖️

Unexplained weight changes

🧬

A changing mole or skin area

⚠️

Persistent unusual symptoms

Important: These changes can have many causes and do not automatically mean cancer. This page cannot diagnose a condition. Seek medical advice for symptoms that concern you or do not go away.
05 — VACCINATION

Vaccines can help prevent some cancers

Vaccination against certain infections is an important part of cancer prevention awareness.

💉

HPV vaccination

HPV vaccination helps prevent infection from HPV types that can cause cervical and some other cancers. Ask a healthcare professional about local recommendations.

🛡️

Hepatitis B vaccination

Hepatitis B vaccination helps prevent infection that can lead to chronic liver disease and increase liver cancer risk.
